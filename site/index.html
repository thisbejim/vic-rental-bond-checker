The detail that changes the number

A month isn’t
four weeks.

Four weeks is 28 days. Consumer Affairs Victoria converts weekly rent to a calendar month using 365 days ÷ 12. That small distinction can change the bond figure by hundreds of dollars.

OFFICIAL CONVERSIONCAV

Daily rent × 365 ÷ 12 = Monthly rent

Example: $550 per week $550 ÷ 7 × 365 ÷ 12 = $2,389.88

The usual Victorian bond limit is one calendar month of rent when rent is $900 a week or less. The same one-month limit generally applies to rent paid in advance.

Quick answers

Good to know

Why is the result different from four weeks of rent?

Four weeks is 28 days. CAV’s rent calculator uses 365 ÷ 12 days for a calendar month, so the usual monthly figure is higher than four weeks of weekly rent.

What if the rent is more than $900 per week?

CAV says the usual one-month bond cap and one-month advance-rent rule do not apply above $900 per week. The checker shows that exception instead of guessing a maximum.

Does this cover rooming houses or caravan parks?

No. Those rental types have different rules. Use the Consumer Affairs Victoria bond guide linked above for the rules that apply to your agreement.

Is this legal advice?

No. It is a calculation aid based on published CAV guidance, last checked on 1 October 2026. It does not account for every agreement or exception. Confirm your situation with CAV or Tenants Victoria.
